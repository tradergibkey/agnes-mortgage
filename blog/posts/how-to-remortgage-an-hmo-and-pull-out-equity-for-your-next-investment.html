How to Remortgage an HMO and Pull Out Equity for Your Next Investment

September 24, 2026 · HMO & Complex Property Investment · Agnes Mortgage

Remortgaging an HMO to release equity: what you need to know

Remortgaging a House in Multiple Occupation (HMO) to release equity is one of the most effective ways to fund your next investment property — but it trips up more landlords than almost any other financing move. The rules differ sharply from standard buy-to-let remortgages, lender appetite is narrower, and the valuation methodology used for HMOs can catch you off guard. Below are the questions landlords ask most often, answered plainly.

Can you actually pull equity out of an HMO, or are lenders reluctant?

You can release equity from an HMO, but the lender pool is smaller than for single-let property. Most high-street banks either decline HMOs outright or cap lending at a level that makes equity release unviable. The lenders who operate comfortably in this space — specialist buy-to-let providers and challenger banks — do permit capital raising, typically up to 75% loan-to-value (LTV), provided the rental income supports the new, higher loan. Whole-of-market brokers working in this niche maintain live relationships with these lenders, which is where access matters most.

How do lenders value an HMO for remortgage purposes?

This is the question most landlords underestimate. HMOs can be valued on two bases: bricks-and-mortar value (what the property would sell for as a standard dwelling) or commercial/investment value (based on the income the property generates). Most specialist lenders instruct surveyors to use the bricks-and-mortar figure, which is often lower than what landlords expect from an income-based calculation. A small number of lenders will use a blended or income-capitalisation approach, particularly for larger HMOs generating strong yields — this can meaningfully increase the available equity. Always confirm the valuation basis before committing to a product.

What loan-to-value can you realistically achieve?

The standard ceiling for HMO remortgages is 75% LTV, with a handful of lenders stretching to 80% for experienced landlords with clean credit. To calculate how much equity you can release, take 75% of the lender's assessed value and subtract your outstanding mortgage balance. If your HMO is valued at £500,000 and you owe £200,000, the maximum loan at 75% LTV is £375,000 — meaning up to £175,000 could be released before costs. The net figure after arrangement fees, legal costs, and early repayment charges on your existing deal determines your real working capital.

How does the rental stress test work for an HMO remortgage?

HMO lenders apply a rental stress test to ensure the rental income covers the mortgage repayments at a notional rate — typically 125%–145% of the pay rate, tested at 5%–6% per annum, depending on whether you hold the property personally or via a limited company. HMOs often generate higher gross rental income than equivalent single-lets, which means they can frequently pass stress tests that a standard buy-to-let property at the same value would fail. Lenders assess the achievable rental income as determined by their surveyor, not necessarily the rent you are currently collecting — so a below-market tenancy can suppress borrowing capacity.

Does it matter whether the HMO is held personally or in a limited company?

Yes, significantly. Limited company HMO remortgages are assessed differently: stress test rates are often more favourable (typically 125% at a 5% notional rate rather than 145%), and the lender list shifts again — some lenders operate exclusively in the personal-name space, others focus entirely on corporate structures. If you are considering moving an HMO from personal ownership into a limited company to remortgage, be aware that this triggers a stamp duty land tax (SDLT) liability and a capital gains tax event in most circumstances. Take tax advice before restructuring ownership purely to access better mortgage terms.

For landlords building portfolios through corporate structures, specialist HMO and multi-unit block finance can be structured to accommodate both equity release and portfolio expansion simultaneously.

What licensing requirements do lenders check before approving the remortgage?

Every HMO lender will ask about licensing. Mandatory HMO licensing applies to properties occupied by five or more people forming two or more households — but many local authorities operate additional or selective licensing schemes that extend requirements further. A lender will typically require sight of a valid HMO licence (or written confirmation from the local authority that one is not required) before issuing a mortgage offer. Submitting an application on an unlicensed HMO is one of the fastest routes to a declined application — and to reputational damage with specialist lenders you may need again in future.

How long does an HMO remortgage typically take to complete?

Allow eight to fourteen weeks from application to completion as a realistic baseline. The primary variables are: surveyor availability (specialist HMO surveyors are in shorter supply than residential valuers), underwriting queues at specialist lenders, and the complexity of your income or corporate structure. Applications through direct-to-lender routes or brokers who submit incomplete cases take longer. A broker who understands the lender's specific requirements — room configurations, licence type, tenancy agreements — and packages the case accurately at submission will materially shorten this timeline.

Can the released equity be used for any purpose, including purchasing the next property?

Lenders do ask for the purpose of capital raising at application, and most specialist HMO lenders permit equity release for property investment — purchasing a further buy-to-let, funding a deposit, or developing an existing asset. What they do not permit is using released equity to fund business operating costs, repay unsecured personal debt in certain structures, or for purposes that would concern a responsible lending assessment. Be straightforward about the purpose: fabricating a reason introduces regulatory risk and, if the lender discovers a discrepancy, it can trigger a full loan recall in extreme cases.

What happens if your existing HMO mortgage has early repayment charges?

Early repayment charges (ERCs) are charged as a percentage of the outstanding loan — commonly 1%–5% depending on how far through the fixed-rate term you are. On a £300,000 HMO mortgage, a 3% ERC costs £9,000. In some cases, the equity you release net of the ERC still makes the move financially sound; in others, it is worth waiting until the fixed period expires before remortgaging. A broker should model both scenarios — remortgaging now versus waiting — including projected rate changes and rental income projections, so you are making a genuinely informed decision.

Does an Article 4 Direction affect your ability to remortgage or raise equity?

Article 4 Directions restrict permitted development rights in designated areas, meaning HMOs in those zones required planning permission to convert. Lenders are aware of Article 4 zones and will check whether the correct consents are in place. An HMO operating without the necessary planning permission in an Article 4 area is effectively a property with a structural legal defect — most lenders will decline, and those who proceed will do so at heavily restricted terms. Confirming your planning position before application avoids a wasted valuation fee and a hard credit search.

Still unsure where to start?

If one question cuts to the heart of most remortgage problems, it is the valuation question: whether your property will be assessed on bricks-and-mortar value or income value determines almost everything else — the maximum loan, the stress test outcome, and the amount of equity you can actually deploy. Before approaching any lender or submitting an application, establish which valuation methodology applies to your specific property, your licence type, and your ownership structure. That single answer will shape every other decision in the process.

Key takeaways

  • HMO remortgages are achievable but require specialist lenders — the high-street market is largely closed to them.
  • Valuation methodology matters: bricks-and-mortar versus income-based valuation directly controls how much equity you can release.
  • 75% LTV is the standard ceiling; your net releasable equity is that figure minus your outstanding balance and all costs.
  • Valid licensing and planning consent are non-negotiable — lenders check both before issuing an offer.
  • Early repayment charges can erode the benefit of remortgaging mid-term; model both the immediate and deferred scenarios before committing.
  • Limited company structures often carry more favourable stress test rates but change the lender landscape and carry tax implications when restructuring.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remortgage my HMO to release equity for a deposit on another property?

Yes, most specialist HMO lenders permit capital raising for property investment purposes, including funding a deposit on a further buy-to-let. You will need to declare the purpose at application, and the new loan must still pass the lender's rental stress test at the higher borrowing level.

How much equity can I release from an HMO remortgage?

The standard maximum is 75% LTV of the lender's assessed value. To calculate your releasable equity, take 75% of the surveyor's valuation, subtract your outstanding mortgage balance, then deduct costs such as arrangement fees, legal fees, and any early repayment charges on your current deal.

Do I need an HMO licence to remortgage?

Yes. Specialist HMO lenders will request a valid HMO licence — or written confirmation from your local authority that one is not required — before issuing a mortgage offer. Operating an unlicensed HMO is one of the most common reasons specialist lenders decline an application.

Is it better to remortgage an HMO personally or through a limited company?

Limited company structures often benefit from lower rental stress test rates (typically 125% versus 145% for personal ownership), which can increase borrowing capacity. However, transferring a personally-held HMO into a company triggers stamp duty and capital gains tax — always take independent tax advice before restructuring for mortgage purposes.

How long does it take to remortgage an HMO?

Allow eight to fourteen weeks from application to completion. The timeline depends on surveyor availability, lender underwriting queues, and how completely your case is packaged at submission. Cases submitted with full documentation — licence, tenancy agreements, accounts — complete faster.
