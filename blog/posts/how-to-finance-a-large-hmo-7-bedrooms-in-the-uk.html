How to Finance a Large HMO (7+ Bedrooms) in the UK

September 17, 2026 · HMO & Complex Property Investment · Agnes Mortgage

Agnes Siry Licensed Mortgage Advisor September 17, 2026

Only a small subset of UK lenders will touch a seven-bedroom HMO — and an even smaller subset will price it competitively. Understanding why, and knowing which doors to knock on, is the difference between a deal that completes and one that stalls for months. By the end of this article, you will know exactly how large HMO finance works, what lenders actually assess, and how to present your case to get the best outcome.

What a Large HMO Is — and Why It Matters to Lenders

A large HMO (House in Multiple Occupation) is a property let to five or more unrelated tenants forming more than one household, requiring a mandatory licence under the Housing Act 2004 — but in property finance, "large HMO" typically refers to properties with seven or more bedrooms, which sit in a commercial lending category entirely separate from standard buy-to-let. Lenders treat these assets differently because the income model, regulatory burden, and property management complexity are categorically different from a single-let or a small HMO.

Why Large HMO Finance Is a Distinct Market Right Now

Demand for HMO rooms has risen sharply since 2022 as rental affordability pressures push more tenants — particularly young professionals and students — toward shared living. Gross yields on well-located large HMOs regularly run at 10–14%, compared to 4–6% on single-let properties in the same postcode. That income premium attracts investors, but it also attracts regulatory scrutiny.

Since the Mandatory Licensing regime was extended in October 2018, any HMO with five or more occupants across at least two households requires a licence regardless of storey count. Local councils layer additional requirements on top — selective licensing, Article 4 directions restricting C4 and Sui Generis use, room-size minima (at least 6.51 m² per single adult room), and fire safety standards. Lenders are acutely aware of these requirements. A property that isn't compliant, or isn't yet licensed, is nearly impossible to finance through mainstream channels.

At the same time, rising base rates since 2022 have tightened HMO stress tests significantly. Where lenders once stress-tested rental income at 125% of the mortgage payment at a notional 5.5%, many now apply 145% at 7.5% or higher for higher-rate taxpayers and limited-company borrowers. That compresses loan-to-value ratios on any given rent roll.

How Lender Criteria Actually Work for 7+ Bedroom HMOs

Licensing and Planning Use Class

Before any lender considers your application, the property must either hold a valid HMO licence or you must demonstrate that one will be granted. Properties with seven or more occupants typically fall under Sui Generis use class rather than C4, meaning they cannot operate as an HMO without specific planning permission in Article 4 areas — and sometimes universally. Lenders will instruct a specialist surveyor who will flag any planning or licensing gap. Resolve it before you apply, not after.

Practically: obtain a copy of any existing licence (confirm its expiry date), confirm the local authority's Article 4 boundary, and check whether the property was lawfully converted. A licensed HMO with clean planning history is vastly easier to finance than one where the status is ambiguous.

Valuation Methodology — Commercial vs. Bricks-and-Mortar

Lenders for large HMOs instruct surveyors who value on a commercial basis — meaning the assessed rental income drives the valuation, not simply comparable house prices. The surveyor will estimate the Estimated Rental Value (ERV), apply a yield-based capitalisation rate, and produce a figure that may be materially different from bricks-and-mortar value. In high-demand cities, this can work in your favour. In weaker markets, a seven-bedroom HMO may value lower than you expect despite the income it generates.

Expect to pay for a full RICS commercial valuation (typically £800–£1,500 for a property of this size). Some lenders require the report to include a fire risk assessment reference and confirm that the room layout meets minimum size requirements.

Loan-to-Value, Stress Tests, and Maximum Loan Size

Most specialist HMO lenders cap LTV at 70–75% of the commercial valuation for properties of this size. A small number of lenders will go to 80% on a case-by-case basis, usually where the borrower has a strong track record and the rent cover is comfortably above the stress test threshold.

Stress tests vary significantly by lender and by borrower tax position. Limited companies currently benefit from slightly lower stress test rates at some lenders — typically 125–130% at a stressed rate of 5.5–7.5% — whereas individuals paying income tax at 40% face 145% or higher coverage requirements. On a seven-bedroom HMO generating £4,200 per month (£50,400 per annum), an individual borrower stress-tested at 145% of 7.5% could support a loan of roughly £450,000–£480,000. Structure matters: the same income through a limited company may support a larger loan at certain lenders.

For specialist HMO and multi-unit block finance, the choice of lender — and how your income is structured — will often be more influential than the headline rate.

Lender Types and Where to Source Finance

High-street banks will not lend on large HMOs as a rule. The market is served by:

  • Specialist BTL and HMO lenders — such as Paragon, Keystone, Foundation Home Loans, Landbay, and Precise Mortgages. These lenders have underwriters experienced in HMO cases and published criteria for properties up to 10 or even 20 bedrooms.
  • Semi-commercial and commercial lenders — where a property tips into full commercial territory (often beyond 6 bedrooms in some lenders' books), commercial mortgage divisions or challenger banks like Together Money, Shawbrook, and Aldermore apply commercial underwriting with more flexibility but typically shorter terms (often interest-only, 3–25 years) and higher arrangement fees.
  • Bridging lenders — useful during conversion, refurbishment, or planning resolution. Bridging finance on an HMO project typically runs at 0.7–1.2% per month, with exit to a term HMO mortgage once the property is licensed and let.

Accessing these lenders directly as a borrower is difficult — most have limited direct-to-consumer operations and prefer applications through whole-of-market brokers familiar with their underwriting quirks.

Borrower Profile and Track Record

For loans above roughly £500,000 — which most large HMO purchases will involve — lenders scrutinise the borrower's experience closely. Underwriters want to see evidence that you understand HMO management: ideally, one or more existing licensed HMOs in your portfolio, or demonstrable professional landlord experience. First-time HMO investors attempting to enter at the seven-bedroom level face a steeper climb; some lenders will simply decline regardless of income coverage.

If you operate through a limited company (an SPV), lenders will assess the directors' personal experience and net worth alongside the company's financials. Most require a personal guarantee from the directors and will stress-test the guarantee against the director's wider portfolio liabilities.

Common Mistakes That Stall Large HMO Finance

  • Applying to the wrong lender first. A refusal from a mainstream lender doesn't just waste time — it leaves a footprint on your credit file that subsequent lenders can see. Research lender appetite before submitting any application.
  • Ignoring licensing timeline. HMO licences typically take 8–16 weeks to process, sometimes longer in busy London boroughs. Completing a purchase before the licence is granted — and before a lender will fund — can leave you bridging at cost unnecessarily.
  • Underestimating the valuation gap. A property purchased for £620,000 may value on commercial methodology at £580,000. If you've budgeted for a 70% LTV loan against purchase price, a lower valuation will require a larger deposit. Always commission a desktop appraisal or seek indicative guidance from a surveyor before exchange.
  • Wrong ownership structure. Some investors purchase in personal name then try to refinance into a limited company — but this triggers stamp duty and capital gains implications. Decide on the holding structure before day one.
  • Treating the stress test as a formality. Running the numbers at the lender's stressed rate before applying is non-negotiable. If the rent roll doesn't cover, no amount of broker skill will make the deal work at that LTV.

A Worked Example: Seven-Bedroom HMO, Birmingham

Consider a semi-detached property in Birmingham's Selly Oak — a strong student area — with seven en-suite rooms. The investor has three existing HMOs in personal name and is purchasing through a newly formed SPV limited company.

  • Purchase price: £540,000
  • Commercial valuation: £525,000 (surveyor applied a 9.2% yield cap rate to ERV of £48,300 per annum)
  • Target LTV: 70% = loan of £367,500
  • Stress test (limited company, 125% at 6.5%): Required annual income = £367,500 × 6.5% × 125% = £29,859. Actual ERV £48,300 — comfortably passes at 161% coverage.
  • Product: Five-year fixed rate at 6.14%, interest-only, through a specialist BTL lender.
  • Monthly payment: Approximately £1,881
  • Net monthly income (after voids and management at 20%): approximately £3,220

The deal works — but only because the limited-company structure unlocked a lower stress-test threshold and the investor's track record satisfied the lender's experience criteria. In personal name, the same application at 145% coverage would have required the loan to be cut to roughly £305,000, increasing the required deposit by over £60,000.

Key Takeaways

  • Large HMOs (7+ bedrooms) sit in a specialist lending category — only a subset of UK lenders will underwrite them, and criteria vary sharply between providers.
  • The property must be licensed and planning-compliant before most lenders will proceed; resolving these issues post-application creates costly delays.
  • Valuations are conducted on a commercial yield basis, not bricks-and-mortar — know your ERV and the local capitalisation rate before you budget.
  • Limited-company ownership often — though not always — unlocks more favourable stress-test thresholds; model both structures before committing to one.
  • Borrower track record is as important as the income figures; lenders want evidence of HMO management experience, especially at higher loan sizes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a mortgage on a 7-bedroom HMO in the UK?

Yes, but not through a high-street bank. Large HMOs with seven or more bedrooms require specialist HMO mortgage lenders such as Paragon, Keystone, Shawbrook, or Together Money, who assess the property on a commercial yield basis. You will also need a valid HMO licence and planning compliance in place before most lenders will proceed.

What LTV is available on a large HMO mortgage?

Most specialist lenders cap LTV at 70–75% of the commercial valuation for large HMOs. A small number will stretch to 80% for experienced landlords with strong rental income coverage. The commercial valuation — based on the property's rental income rather than comparable house prices — determines the loan ceiling, not the purchase price.

Is it better to buy a large HMO in a limited company or personal name?

For most investors, a limited company SPV structure offers tax efficiency advantages, particularly around mortgage interest deductibility. It also often unlocks lower stress-test thresholds at certain lenders, which can increase the maximum available loan. However, the right structure depends on your wider tax position and portfolio plans — take specialist advice before committing.

How does the rental stress test work for HMO mortgages?

HMO lenders calculate whether the property's rental income sufficiently covers the mortgage payment at a stressed interest rate. A typical calculation requires the annual rent to equal at least 125–145% of the annual mortgage payment calculated at a notional rate of 5.5–7.5%. Individual borrowers paying higher-rate income tax face higher coverage requirements than limited companies at many lenders.

Do I need an HMO licence before applying for a mortgage?

In most cases, yes. Specialist HMO lenders require the property to hold — or be eligible to receive — a valid mandatory HMO licence before they will approve and release funds. Licensing applications can take 8–16 weeks, so factoring this into your timeline before exchange of contracts is essential to avoid costly bridging arrangements.
