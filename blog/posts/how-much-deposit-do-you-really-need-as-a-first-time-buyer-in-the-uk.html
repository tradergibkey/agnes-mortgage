How Much Deposit Do You Really Need as a First-Time Buyer in the UK?

October 10, 2026 · First time buyers · Agnes Mortgage

Agnes Siry Licensed Mortgage Advisor October 10, 2026

Most first-time buyers in the UK can purchase with a 5% deposit, but the deposit you need and the deposit you should have are two different numbers — and confusing them can cost you tens of thousands of pounds in interest over the life of your mortgage.

By the end of this guide, you will know exactly how to calculate your target deposit, which schemes can top it up, and how to stress-test the whole figure before you make an offer. You will need a rough idea of the property price you're targeting, your current savings balance, and about fifteen minutes.

Step 1: Understand What the Minimum Actually Means

The legal minimum deposit for a residential mortgage in the UK is 5% of the purchase price. On a £300,000 property, that is £15,000. Most high-street lenders offer 95% loan-to-value (LTV) products, and since the Mortgage Guarantee Scheme was extended to 2025, you have a reasonable number of lenders to choose from at that tier.

What the 5% figure does not tell you is the cost. At 95% LTV, you will pay a materially higher interest rate than at 90%, 85%, or 80% LTV. The difference between a 95% LTV rate and an 85% LTV rate can easily be 0.75–1.25 percentage points. On a £250,000 mortgage over 25 years, that gap translates to roughly £150–£200 more per month — and a total interest bill that is £40,000–£60,000 higher over the full term.

The minimum deposit gets you through the door. It does not get you the best deal.

Step 2: Map the LTV Tiers That Unlock Better Rates

Lenders price their products in bands. Crossing into a lower LTV band — even by £1 — typically unlocks a cheaper rate across the entire mortgage. The key thresholds to target are:

  • 95% LTV — minimum entry; highest rates, fewest lenders
  • 90% LTV — significant rate improvement; much wider lender choice
  • 85% LTV — noticeably cheaper again; most mainstream lenders compete hard here
  • 80% LTV — often the sweet spot for balance between deposit size and rate reduction
  • 75% LTV — near-best rates available; competitive across the whole market

Work backwards from the property price you can realistically afford. If a property costs £280,000, a 10% deposit (£28,000) puts you at 90% LTV. An extra £14,000 saved — reaching £42,000 — drops you to 85% LTV and unlocks meaningfully lower rates. Map these numbers on paper before you commit to a savings target.

To understand exactly how residential mortgages are structured at each LTV tier and what rates are currently available to first-time buyers, it helps to model multiple scenarios with someone who has access to the full market.

Step 3: Account for All the Costs Beyond the Deposit

Your deposit is not your only upfront cost. First-time buyers frequently underestimate the additional expenses — and running out of cash at completion can collapse a purchase entirely. Budget for the following on top of your deposit:

  • Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) — first-time buyers pay 0% on the first £425,000 (on properties up to £625,000 purchase price). From April 2025, this threshold reverts to £300,000, so check the current rates at the point you buy.
  • Solicitor / conveyancing fees — typically £1,200–£2,500 depending on complexity and location.
  • Survey costs — a basic valuation is often lender-arranged, but a HomeBuyer Report (Level 2) costs £400–£900; a full structural survey (Level 3) runs £600–£1,500.
  • Mortgage arrangement and broker fees — lenders sometimes charge product fees of £999–£2,000 (these can usually be added to the loan, though that increases interest). Specialist brokers typically charge £995–£2,500 flat for complex cases; Agnes Mortgage works on a flat £500 per case.
  • Removal costs — £500–£2,000 depending on distance and volume.
  • Initial furnishing and repairs — budget at least £2,000–£5,000 unless the property is fully fitted.

A reasonable rule of thumb: add 3–4% of the purchase price on top of your deposit to cover all purchase costs. On a £300,000 property, that means a further £9,000–£12,000 in liquid cash.

Step 4: Explore Schemes That Can Supplement Your Deposit

If your savings fall short of the LTV tier you are targeting, the following schemes can make up the shortfall or reduce the loan amount:

  • Lifetime ISA (LISA) — save up to £4,000 per year; the government adds a 25% bonus (up to £1,000/year). Open before age 40; must have been open at least 12 months before you use it. Property price cap: £450,000.
  • Shared Ownership — buy a share (typically 25–75%) of a property and pay rent on the rest. Reduces the deposit required because you only finance your share. You can buy further shares over time ('staircasing').
  • First Homes scheme — eligible first-time buyers and key workers can purchase new-build homes at a 30–50% discount from market value. Discount stays with the property on resale.
  • Bank of Family — gifted deposits from parents or family members are accepted by most lenders. The donor must confirm in writing that the gift carries no obligation to repay. Some lenders also offer 'joint borrower, sole proprietor' products where a parent's income supports the mortgage without their name on the title deeds.
  • Mortgage Guarantee Scheme — allows lenders to offer 95% LTV mortgages backed by a government guarantee. You still need the 5% deposit; the scheme is for the lender's benefit, not a direct subsidy to you.

None of these schemes are automatic. Each has eligibility criteria, property price caps, and timing constraints. Apply for a LISA as early as possible — the 12-month waiting rule is non-negotiable.

Step 5: Stress-Test Your Deposit Against Lender Affordability

A deposit is only half the conversation. Lenders assess affordability separately, and even a 20% deposit will not compensate for a loan that fails the income multiple test. Most high-street lenders cap borrowing at 4–4.5 times your gross annual income. Some specialist lenders go to 5–5.5x for higher earners or specific professions.

Check both variables together:

  • Does your deposit (plus scheme support) reach the LTV tier you are targeting?
  • Does the resulting loan amount sit within 4.5x your provable income?
  • Does the monthly repayment pass a stress test at a rate 2–3% above the product rate?

If the numbers fail on affordability, increasing your deposit further reduces the loan and can bring the payment back within limits. If they fail on income, your options are a lower-priced property, a longer term (with care — you will pay more interest), or a joint application.

If It Goes Wrong: Common Deposit Pitfalls and How to Recover

Even well-prepared buyers hit obstacles. Here are the most common problems and the practical fixes:

  • Savings fall short at the last minute — if the property valuation comes in lower than the agreed purchase price, the lender calculates LTV on the lower figure. Your deposit may suddenly be insufficient. Solution: renegotiate the purchase price, make up the shortfall in cash, or walk away.
  • Gifted deposit not properly documented — lenders require a signed gift letter and, increasingly, evidence of the donor's source of funds. Missing paperwork can delay completion by weeks. Have this ready before you instruct solicitors.
  • LISA bonus not yet released — you cannot use a LISA that has not been open 12 months. If your timeline is tight, complete with other savings and keep the LISA invested.
  • Mortgage in principle (MIP) issued at one LTV, actual application at another — estate agents treat MIPs as a sign of buying intent, not a guarantee of the rate. Always confirm the actual rate band with a broker before exchanging contracts.
  • Deposit sitting in multiple accounts flagged by AML checks — anti-money laundering rules require you to evidence where every pound of your deposit originated. Consolidate your savings into one account at least 3 months before applying and keep a clear audit trail.

How to Know Your Deposit Plan Is Working

You will know your deposit strategy is on track when three conditions are simultaneously true:

  1. Your cash savings — plus any scheme contributions — reach at least the next meaningful LTV tier above the minimum, with 3–4% of the purchase price left over for purchase costs.
  2. The loan amount that results sits within 4.5x your provable gross income (or the specific multiple a broker has confirmed for your situation).
  3. A lender has issued a Decision in Principle at that exact LTV tier — not just at the minimum — confirming that both your deposit and income pass their current criteria.

When all three boxes are ticked simultaneously, you are not just a buyer in theory. You are a buyer in practice, with a competitive rate locked in, a deal that survives a valuation wobble, and enough cash left to actually move.

Key Takeaways

  • The minimum UK deposit for a first-time buyer is 5%, but 10% or 15% typically unlocks significantly better rates and can save £40,000+ over the mortgage term.
  • LTV banding matters: 95%, 90%, 85%, 80%, and 75% are the main pricing thresholds — crossing each one downwards usually reduces your rate.
  • Budget 3–4% of the purchase price on top of your deposit for solicitor fees, surveys, and other purchase costs.
  • Lifetime ISAs, Shared Ownership, First Homes, and gifted deposits can all supplement your own savings — but each has eligibility rules and timing constraints.
  • Affordability and deposit are assessed separately; even a large deposit cannot compensate for a loan that exceeds lender income multiples.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum deposit for a first-time buyer in the UK?

The minimum deposit for most UK mortgage lenders is 5% of the property purchase price. On a £250,000 home, that is £12,500. However, 5% LTV products carry the highest interest rates, so saving a 10% or 15% deposit will typically save you significantly more in interest over the mortgage term.

Is a 10% deposit enough to buy a house in the UK as a first-time buyer?

Yes — a 10% deposit (90% LTV) is accepted by the majority of mainstream UK lenders and unlocks noticeably better rates than the minimum 5%. You will also need an additional 3–4% of the purchase price in cash to cover legal fees, surveys, and other purchase costs, so factor those in separately.

Can I use a Lifetime ISA as a deposit for my first home?

Yes, but the Lifetime ISA must have been open for at least 12 months before you use it, and the property must cost no more than £450,000. The government adds a 25% bonus on up to £4,000 of savings per year, giving you up to £1,000 extra annually. Withdraw it for any other purpose and you face a 25% penalty that effectively wipes out the bonus and eats into your own savings.

Do first-time buyers pay Stamp Duty in the UK?

First-time buyers currently pay 0% Stamp Duty (SDLT) on properties up to £425,000 and a reduced rate on the portion between £425,001 and £625,000. From April 2025, this relief reverts to the standard first-time buyer threshold of £300,000, so the timing of your purchase can affect your upfront costs significantly.

Can my parents gift me a deposit for a mortgage?

Yes — most UK mortgage lenders accept gifted deposits from parents or close family members. The donor must sign a gift letter confirming the money is a gift and not a loan, and lenders increasingly require evidence of the source of funds to satisfy anti-money laundering rules. Some lenders also offer joint borrower, sole proprietor mortgages where a parent's income supports the application without them owning a share of the property.
